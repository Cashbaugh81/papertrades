# Kill Test v1 — the cheapest test of the assumption most likely to be false

## What to test: A1 (retention without cash incentive)
Not A4 (feature defensibility), not A5 (CAC). Those are expensive to learn and, if A1 is false, irrelevant.

## Design: concierge, no product, two weeks
Find 12-15 people who match your target user exactly: 22-35, has investable cash sitting idle or in a
savings account, has *considered* buying individual stocks in the last 90 days but hasn't, and is not
already running a brokerage account with more than a token balance.

For each, by hand:
1. Ask their cash and monthly contribution.
2. Build three parallel portfolios *for them* — one boring ETF mix, one theme-concentrated, one stock-picked.
3. Send a weekly one-page "here's how your three would have done" note, Sunday evening. Same dollars,
   three paths, drawn in a spreadsheet. No app.
4. Offer nothing else. No lessons, no gamification, no reminders between weeks.

## What you measure
- Week 4 reply rate. Does the person still open it and *say something back*?
- Whether they ask a question you did not prompt ("wait, why is the chip one down so much?")
- Whether, unprompted, they ask to put real money somewhere.
- Whether they ask to keep it going after week 4.

## Kill criteria
- Fewer than 5 of 12 still engaging at week 4 → the loop does not hold attention. Stop, or find a version
  with a much faster payoff (daily, not weekly; or one decision, not a portfolio).
- People engage but never ask about real money → you have a toy, not a bridge to investing. The "urges"
  promise is not landing.
- People engage and ask "can you just tell me what to buy" → you have an advice product wearing an
  education costume, which is a licensing problem, not a design problem.

## Cost and time
2-3 hours per participant per week for four weeks. Zero engineering. This is the cheapest possible
falsification of the thing that would kill the business.

## The trap to avoid
Do not build a prototype first. A prototype will get polite engagement from friends and tell you nothing,
and it will cost you six weeks you could have spent learning whether anyone comes back in week four.
